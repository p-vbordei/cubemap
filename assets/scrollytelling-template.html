chapter one · the opportunity & the danger

Money lives in the spread. So does the risk.

Power is cheap when the sun floods the grid at midday and dear in the evening peak. A battery earns by buying low and selling high — but every cycle wears the cells and pushes against a hard grid limit. The entire business is that spread, captured without ever crossing a line.

chapter two · the idea

One small loop. Nothing acts ungated.

The machine is a single governed loop: it senses the plant, decides a move, then the move must pass three gates — is it sane, is it allowed, is it authorized — before it acts. Every step is signed onto an audit trail you can replay. Deny by default; an action with no receipt never happened.

chapter three · how it stays honest

Every edge has a stand-in.

Each thing that touches the real world — an inverter, a price feed, a forecast — is a seam: a tiny contract with a deterministic stand-in (a Fake) as its source of truth. The whole system runs and tests offline, with no keys and no hardware. The real vendor is just one plug behind the same shape — so FusionSolar is one adapter, never the design.

chapter four · the rules, made physical

Illegal states simply can't be reached.

The battery's life is a tiny state machine, and the business rules are its guards. It cannot enter Discharging if the charge is below the floor, or if the spread doesn't beat the wear. Trip a safety limit or pull the kill-switch and it falls closed to Idle. Compliance isn't a checklist — it's the shape of the machine.

chapter five · the twist

It was never one cube.

To decide well, the battery needs to see the future: tomorrow's prices, its solar, the load. Each of those is its own engine — and we build them. So the system is a constellation: forecasting cubes that are products in their own right, feeding one dispatch cube that acts on the asset and the market. All on the same spine.

chapter six · how it holds together

A thin brain over deep engines.

The engines are real services — Rust, Python, TypeScript — with their own databases and queues. They sit in a capability plane. Above them, a thin control plane (the cubes, the gates, the spine) does the governing. The seam is the border between them: a Fake on the inside for testing, a real adapter reaching the engine in production.

chapter seven · a day in the life

Plan once. Steer ninety-six times.

Just after prices publish, the forecasts converge into one optimal plan for tomorrow. Then, every fifteen minutes, the cube reads the battery, computes the nudge that hits the planned charge, clears the gates, and acts — settling each interval onto the record.

chapter eight · shipping it

One project. One public door.

On Railway, only the conductor faces the world — the MCP control surface and the reports. Every engine and datastore lives on the private network behind it, reached with a short-lived minted token. The whole thing builds into a single bundle and tests green before a single real service is wired.

chapter nine · the path forward

Stand-ins first. One green step at a time.

We write the contracts, then their Fakes, then the cube around them — proving a full day offline before a single real service is touched. Each step is green on its own. We're done when every capability has a stand-in and a real plug, all wired in the conductor, deployable.

Where we are: the design is complete, end to end. No code yet. The build begins at P0 — and from there, every functionality becomes a cube, until the battery runs itself.
